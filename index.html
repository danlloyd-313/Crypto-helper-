    What everything means, in plain English

    Market mood is the Crypto Fear & Greed Index. It blends price swings, trading volume, social media and surveys into one 0–100 score. Very low scores mean most people are scared and many have already sold, which is often near a low.

    7-month average is the average price over the last 200 days. Below it, the trend is down. When a coin climbs back above it and stays there, the trend has usually turned.

    4-year average is Bitcoin's average weekly price over about four years (the 200-week average). Every past bear market has bottomed near or just under it.

    Selling looks overdone uses a measure of how one-sided the last 14 days were (RSI under 30). When almost every day has been down, sellers are often close to running out.

    Entry score is 55% how cheap the coin is against its all-time high, 25% whether it's turning up, and 20% the market backdrop. A high score means the price and timing look good. It says nothing about whether the project itself is any good, which is why the risk label sits next to it.

    Simulations shuffle each coin's real daily price moves from the past year into 2,000 possible paths, then nudge them up or down for the economy you pick. The past-recovery lines show what Bitcoin actually did after its 2019 and 2022 bottoms, applied to today's price.

    None of this can tell you the exact bottom, and markets can stay fearful for months. It's information to help you decide, not financial advice.
